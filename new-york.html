New York City attraction tickets & city passes

Observation decks, the Statue of Liberty, world-famous museums and Broadway: what to book ahead, what is free, and how to tell whether a New York city pass is worth it.

Top New York attractions to book

These are the attractions most visitors plan a New York trip around, with what to check before you pay.

  • Empire State BuildingClassic Art Deco observatory on the 86th floor, with an optional higher deck. Sunset slots sell out first.
  • Top of the Rock & EdgeTwo other major observation decks. Each needs a timed ticket; compare views and time slots before you pick one.
  • Statue of Liberty & Ellis IslandReached only by the official ferry from Battery Park or Liberty State Park. Pedestal and crown access is limited, so book early and avoid unofficial street sellers.
  • The Metropolitan Museum of ArtOne of the world's largest art museums. Check the current admission policy on the museum's site.
  • American Museum of Natural HistoryDinosaur halls, the planetarium and special exhibitions, which may be priced separately.
  • 9/11 Memorial & MuseumThe outdoor memorial pools are free; the museum needs a timed ticket.
  • Broadway showsBuy from the theater box office or official sellers. Same-day lotteries and discount booths can save money.
  • MoMAModern and contemporary art in Midtown. Timed entry is used on busy days.
Help: Hours, prices and booking rules change by season. Always confirm on the attraction's official site or the partner checkout page. Official information: NYC Tourism (official visitor guide) ↗ and Statue of Liberty National Monument (NPS) ↗.

Is a New York city pass worth it?

  • Count your paid stops. Passes usually break even at around three paid attractions. If your list is mostly free sights, skip the pass.
  • Check the clock. Many passes run for consecutive days from first use. Plan two or three attractions a day, not five.
  • Read reservation rules. Some included attractions still need a timed booking even with a pass.
  • Compare single tickets. For one or two big attractions, direct tickets are often cheaper.

Getting around and saving money

  • Use the subway. Tap-to-pay works on subways and buses. Check current MTA fares ↗.
  • Book timed entry early for the most popular attractions, especially on weekends and holidays.
  • Mix free and paid. Pair a free sight in the morning with one paid attraction in the afternoon.
  • Arrive at opening time for the shortest lines. See how to skip lines at popular attractions.

New York tickets FAQ

Is a New York city pass worth it?

Only if you will visit three or more paid attractions in a few days. Add up the gate prices of the places you really plan to see and compare that with the pass price.

How do I avoid fake Statue of Liberty tickets?

The only boats that land on Liberty and Ellis Islands are the official ferries. Book through the official ferry operator or an authorized seller, not street vendors.

Which New York observation deck is best?

It depends on the view you want. The Empire State Building is the classic, Top of the Rock gives a view that includes the Empire State Building, and Edge has an outdoor glass floor. Pick by time slot and budget.
