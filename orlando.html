Orlando attraction tickets & city passes

Theme parks, the Kennedy Space Center and family attractions: how to choose Orlando tickets, avoid overpaying, and plan days that are not all spent in lines.

Top Orlando attractions to book

These are the attractions most visitors plan a Orlando trip around, with what to check before you pay.

  • Walt Disney WorldFour theme parks plus water parks. Prices change by date, and park reservation rules can change, so check the official site before you buy.
  • Universal Orlando ResortTheme parks and a water park. Multi-park and express options cost more but can save hours in peak season.
  • SeaWorld OrlandoMarine park with coasters and shows. Look for multi-park bundles if you also plan Aquatica or Busch Gardens.
  • Kennedy Space Center Visitor ComplexAbout an hour east of Orlando. Plan a full day and check launch-viewing options.
  • ICON ParkObservation wheel, attractions and dining on International Drive. Combo tickets are common.
  • GatorlandLong-running wildlife park with alligator shows and a zip line.
  • Lake Eola ParkFree downtown park with the swan boats and a weekly farmers market.
  • Water parksSeveral large water parks run seasonally. Check closures in the cooler months.
Help: Hours, prices and booking rules change by season. Always confirm on the attraction's official site or the partner checkout page. Official information: Visit Orlando (official visitor guide) ↗ and Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex ↗.

Is a Orlando city pass worth it?

  • Count your paid stops. Passes usually break even at around three paid attractions. If your list is mostly free sights, skip the pass.
  • Check the clock. Many passes run for consecutive days from first use. Plan two or three attractions a day, not five.
  • Read reservation rules. Some included attractions still need a timed booking even with a pass.
  • Compare single tickets. For one or two big attractions, direct tickets are often cheaper.

Getting around and saving money

  • Plan for a car or shuttles. Attractions are spread out; many hotels run park shuttles and rideshare is common. See LYNX public bus routes ↗.
  • Book timed entry early for the most popular attractions, especially on weekends and holidays.
  • Mix free and paid. Pair a free sight in the morning with one paid attraction in the afternoon.
  • Arrive at opening time for the shortest lines. See how to skip lines at popular attractions.

Orlando tickets FAQ

Where should I buy Orlando theme park tickets?

From the park's official site or an authorized ticket seller. Avoid resold or used tickets: most are tied to one person and may be refused at the gate.

How many days do I need in Orlando?

Most families plan one day per major park, plus at least one rest or pool day. Multi-day tickets usually lower the per-day price.

When are Orlando parks least crowded?

Outside school holidays, typically mid-January to early February, late April to May, and September. Check park calendars for events that draw crowds.
