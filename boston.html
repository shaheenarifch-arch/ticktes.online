Boston attraction tickets & city passes

The Freedom Trail, world-class museums, the aquarium and the harbor: what to book, what is free, and how to decide whether a Boston city pass actually saves you money.

Top Boston attractions to book

Most visitors build a Boston trip around a few anchor attractions. Here is what each one involves and what to check before you pay.

  • Freedom TrailA free, self-guided 2.5-mile walk past 16 historic sites, marked by a red line from Boston Common to Charlestown. Guided tours are paid.
  • Museum of Fine ArtsOne of the largest art museums in the US. Check timed-entry rules and any free-admission days on the museum's site.
  • Isabella Stewart Gardner MuseumA Venetian-style palazzo with a courtyard garden. Entry is timed, so book ahead on busy days.
  • New England AquariumWaterfront aquarium with a giant ocean tank. Weekends and school holidays sell out early, so pick an entry time.
  • Museum of ScienceHands-on exhibits, planetarium and theater shows. Shows are often priced separately from exhibit halls.
  • Boston Tea Party Ships & MuseumInteractive history experience on the harbor. Tours run at set times.
  • Fenway Park toursBallpark tours of the home of the Red Sox. Game-day schedules can change tour times.
  • Harbor cruises & whale watchingSeasonal boat trips from Long Wharf. Check weather policies and refund terms before booking.
Help: Opening hours and prices change by season. Always confirm on the attraction's official site or the partner checkout page. For official visitor information see Meet Boston (official visitor guide) ↗ and the National Park Service page for Boston National Historical Park ↗.

Is a Boston city pass worth it?

Boston has multi-attraction passes that bundle entry to places like the aquarium, museums, harbor cruises and observation decks. They can save money, but only if you use them enough.

  • Count your paid stops. Passes usually break even at around three paid attractions. If your list is mostly free sights (Freedom Trail, Boston Common, Public Garden), skip the pass.
  • Check the clock. Many passes run for consecutive days from first use. Plan two or three attractions per day, not five.
  • Read reservation rules. Some included attractions still need a timed booking even with a pass.
  • Compare single tickets. If you only want one or two big attractions, buying those tickets directly is often cheaper.

Getting around and saving money

  • Use the T. The subway reaches most attractions; the Green Line runs near the Museum of Fine Arts and Fenway. Check current MBTA fares ↗.
  • Walk the core. Downtown, the North End, Beacon Hill and the waterfront are close together.
  • Book timed entry early for the aquarium, Gardner Museum and weekend harbor cruises.
  • Mix free and paid. Pair a free Freedom Trail morning with one paid museum in the afternoon.

Boston tickets FAQ

Is a Boston city pass worth it?

A pass usually pays off only if you will visit three or more paid attractions in a short window. Add up the gate prices of the places you actually plan to see and compare that with the pass price before you buy.

Is the Freedom Trail free?

Walking the Freedom Trail is free. It is marked by a red line on the sidewalk. Some sites along the route charge admission, and guided walking tours are paid.

How do I get around Boston without a car?

Most central attractions are walkable or reachable by the MBTA subway (the T). Check the MBTA site for current fares and service changes before your trip.

When is the best time to visit Boston attractions?

Late spring and early fall usually offer comfortable weather. Summer and school holidays are busiest, so book timed-entry tickets early and arrive at opening time for shorter lines.
