How to choose city passes

City passes commonly use either a time-based model, such as consecutive hours or days, or an attraction-count model. The better format depends on trip length, pace and the exact places visitors want to enter.

An included attraction may still require a reservation. Popular time slots can fill, so check the reservation process before purchasing and reserve eligible visits promptly after receiving the pass.

What to check before booking

  • Pass model and validity
  • Included attractions
  • Reservation procedure
  • Public transport or sightseeing transport
  • Activation and expiry
  • Child and family pricing
  • Digital or physical pass collection
  • Unused-attraction and refund rules

Build a better comparison

Transport language needs careful reading. A pass may include public transportation, a sightseeing bus, selected transfers or no transportation at all. Confirm zones, operating hours and activation rules.

Build a day-by-day itinerary using real opening hours and travel time, then compare the likely individual prices with the pass cost. Leave room for meals, weather and unscheduled exploration.

Decision checklist

Compare the details that change the day.

A low headline price is not automatically better if the usable dates, included access or cancellation rules do not fit the plan.

FAQ

City Passes questions

What is a city pass?

A city pass bundles access to attractions and sometimes transportation or tours under one product and validity period.

Do city passes guarantee entry?

Not necessarily. Some attractions require advance reservations or have capacity limits even when admission is included.

When should a city pass be activated?

Activation rules vary. Some start at first use, while others begin on a selected date or immediately after redemption.

How can visitors decide if a city pass is good value?

List the attractions that realistically fit the schedule, calculate their current individual prices and compare the total with the pass price and restrictions.
